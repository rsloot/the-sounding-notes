# AI Shopping Agents Could Mean Better Markets and Worse Institutions: data notes

Article: [The Sounding](https://thesounding.substack.com), November 24, 2026.

The piece is a thought experiment: it argues from a premise about a world where AI agents do most consumer transactions. The sources below are the ideas it builds on, the evidence it cites as signals, and the prior work it credits. Quotations are from the editions named.

## Hirschman

- Albert O. Hirschman, *Exit, Voice, and Loyalty* (Harvard University Press, 1970).
- Albert O. Hirschman, *The Passions and the Interests* (Princeton University Press, 1977).
- Albert O. Hirschman, "Rival Interpretations of Market Society: Civilizing, Destructive, or Feeble?" *Journal of Economic Literature* 20(4), 1982, pp. 1463-84.

## Montesquieu, Smith and *doux commerce*

- Montesquieu, *De l'esprit des lois* (1748), Book XX, chapter 1 (1777 edition, Wikisource): "Le commerce guérit des préjugés destructeurs ; & c'est presque une règle générale, que par-tout où il y a des mœurs douces, il y a du commerce ; & que par-tout où il y a du commerce, il y a des mœurs douces." Same chapter: "le commerce corrompt les mœurs pures" and "polit & adoucit les mœurs barbares."
- "Doux commerce" as a label was popularized by Hirschman.
- Adam Smith on probity and punctuality: *Lectures on Jurisprudence* (paraphrased in the article, not quoted).
- David Hume, *A Treatise of Human Nature*, Book 3, Part 2, Section 5 ("Of the obligation of promises"), on how self-interested exchange teaches people to keep promises: "Hence I learn to do a service to another, without bearing him any real kindness; because I forsee, that he will return my service" (T 3.2.5.9, Hume Texts Online, davidhume.org).
- David Hume, "Of Refinement in the Arts" (*Political Discourses*, 1752): "The more these refined arts advance, the more sociable men become"; "industry, knowledge, and humanity, are linked together by an indissoluble chain" (RA 5, Hume Texts Online).

## Market integration and fairness

- Joseph Henrich et al., "In Search of Homo Economicus: Behavioral Experiments in 15 Small-Scale Societies," *American Economic Review*, 2001.
- Joseph Henrich et al., "Markets, Religion, Community Size, and the Evolution of Fairness and Punishment," *Science* 327:1480-84 (2010): 15 populations; Dictator, Ultimatum and Third-Party Punishment games; market integration measured by the share of calories purchased.
- Both studies are correlational, as the article says.

## Prior work credited

- Michal S. Gal and Niva Elkin-Koren, "Algorithmic Consumers," *Harvard Journal of Law and Technology* 30:309 (2017). The aside on the social side of shopping: "How will they be affected by the loss of the social interactions that often accompany shopping? Such matters are beyond our expertise."
- Rory Van Loo, "Digital Market Perfection," 117 *Mich. L. Rev.* 815 (2019). Hyperswitching and market volatility (abstract, p. 815); "a new type of bank run—one that is technologically coordinated rather than driven by panic" (p. 821); banks blocking the bots of financial apps such as NerdWallet (pp. 838-39). It cites Hirschman twice, both on exit and market concentration (nn. 149, 220), and makes no argument about voice or social norms.
- Mark Bartholomew and Samuel I. Becher, "The End of Shopping," 68 *Wm. & Mary L. Rev.* 1 (2026).
- Joshua Gans, Avi Goldfarb and Mara Lederman, "Exit, Tweets and Loyalty" (NBER Working Paper w23046), on Hirschman and platforms.
- Digital Journal (October 29, 2026), on firms' own customer-service bots and voice.
- Marketers on AI agents and brand loyalty, for example Matt Britton, "AI Shopping Agents Are Quietly Killing Brand Loyalty Programs" (mattbritton.com, August 12, 2026).

## Forecast check

- By the end of 2030, at least one of the ten largest U.S. banks will allow customers' third-party AI agents to initiate transfers out of their accounts to another institution without the customer approving each transfer. Wrong if none of the ten does.
